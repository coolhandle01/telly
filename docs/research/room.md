# The room

The set does not float in a void. It stands in a sitting room, and the scene it
needs follows from what kind of television it is.

## It stands on the floor

This is a **console**: it has legs and a deep apron, and it *is* the furniture.
So it wants a wall behind it, a skirting board, and carpet — not a sideboard. A
portable would want the sideboard, which is why `Cabinet` takes `legs={false}`;
the day that set exists, the room gets a piece of furniture instead of a floor.

The floor line is the stage's own bottom edge, which is where the feet are. No
measurement, no magic number: the set stands on the carpet at every window size
because the carpet starts where the set stops.

## The room is dim, and that is doing work

A sitting room with the curtains shut and the only real light coming off the
tube. That is what it looked like, and it is also what stops the wallpaper
competing with the television — which is the thing anyone came for. A bright
room would win the picture and lose the point.

Everything in it is lit from the **upper left**, like every surface on the set.
The light falls across the paper from that corner and dies towards the others.

## What is in it

**Wallpaper.** Interlocking rings on a staggered tile, the pattern a 1970
sitting room had and nobody chose twice. Two rings per tile offset by half a
step, which is what makes it read as hung paper rather than as a grid.

**Skirting.** Painted, and the first version was wrong: gloss cream at full
value made a bright band straight across the frame and pulled the eye off the
television. It sits at floor level in a room lit from above, so it is the
dimmest paint in the house, not the brightest thing in the picture.

**Carpet.** Dark, warm, short pile, going darker towards the front of the room
where the light does not reach — which is also what keeps the floor from
reading as a wall lying down. The set pools a shadow onto it where it stands.

## Not yet built
