Privacy policy

telly · last updated 26 September 2026

telly builds a day’s television schedule out of the YouTube channels you subscribe to, and plays the programmes in YouTube’s own player. This policy covers the version of telly published at telly.na-n.xyz. It explains what telly receives from your Google and YouTube accounts, what it does with it, how it is protected, how long it is kept, and how to remove it.

Who is responsible for telly

telly is made and published by one person as a hobby project. There is no company behind it, no telly account to create, and no server of telly’s own. telly runs inside your browser. The person who publishes telly receives none of the information telly reads from your Google and YouTube accounts.

In short

  • telly asks Google for one permission, which Google describes as “View your YouTube account”. telly only reads with it.
  • What it reads goes from Google to your browser. telly has no server, so none of it is sent to us, and we hold no copy of it.
  • It is used only to build the schedule you watch.
  • We do not sell it or use it for advertising, and telly passes none of it to anyone but Google and YouTube.
  • Signing out hands the permission back to Google and removes what telly saved on your device.

The permission you give

When you sign in, Google asks you to grant telly one permission:

https://www.googleapis.com/auth/youtube.readonly

Google describes it as “View your YouTube account”. telly uses it only to read: your channel identifier, the channels you subscribe to, and the videos those channels have uploaded. telly does not post, comment, subscribe, unsubscribe or delete anything.

What telly reads from your YouTube account

  • Your account’s channel identifier, so that telly can tell whose saved copy is whose.
  • The channels you subscribe to, up to 1,000 of them, and their names.
  • For each of those channels: the topics YouTube lists for it, and its subscriber count when YouTube gives one.
  • For each of those channels, up to twenty of its uploads: the title, how long it runs, when it was published, its category and tags, its view count, whether YouTube has age-restricted it, whether it is designated as made for children, whether it can be embedded on other websites, and whether it is a live or upcoming broadcast.

YouTube’s answers also carry some details telly does not use, such as the date you subscribed to each channel, video descriptions and like counts. telly discards those without saving them. It reads nothing else from your account, and it does not read your watch history.

How telly uses it

telly sorts the channels you subscribe to between five stations, and gives each station a day of programmes. What it reads decides which station a channel belongs to, and what time of day its uploads go out. The schedule is built in your browser and shown to you. Your channel identifier is used to file the saved copy described below. That is the whole of what the information is used for.

telly keeps no record of what you watch. It uses none of this information for advertising, and none of it to train any model.

Who it is shared with

Nobody but Google and YouTube, and them in one way only: when a programme starts, telly tells YouTube’s player which video to play. Otherwise what telly reads goes from Google to your browser, and telly sends it no further. telly has no server to send it on to and no database outside your browser to put it in, so no copy reaches the person who publishes telly, and none of it is sold. The companies involved in running telly at all are listed under “Other companies involved”, below.

What is kept on your device, and for how long

  • The saved copy. What telly reads from YouTube is saved in this site’s storage in your browser, filed under your channel identifier, so that switching the set on again does not have to fetch it all a second time. telly uses a saved copy for up to 24 hours. After that it fetches a new copy the next time you use it, and saves that in place of the old one. A saved copy is not deleted when it is 24 hours old. It stays on your device until a new copy replaces it, you sign out, or you clear this site’s data. Your browser may also delete it on its own to free space.
  • Your sign-in. When you sign in, Google gives telly a pass that lets it read your YouTube account for a limited time. Google sets that time, and tells telly what it is when it issues the pass. telly keeps the pass in this tab’s own storage, so that reloading the page does not sign you out. It goes when you close the tab, although a browser that reopens closed tabs may bring it back with the tab. telly stops using the pass a minute before its time runs out, and will not take up a restored one whose time has run out. telly notices that the time has run out the next time it needs to ask YouTube for something, or when the page is reloaded, and returns to its signed-out state then. Until that happens the page can go on showing you as signed in after the pass has run out.

telly itself stores nothing else on your device and sets no cookie. Google’s sign-in software and YouTube’s player software run as part of the page, and what they store on this site is up to Google and YouTube. Your browser may also keep a copy of the page itself in its cache.

How your information is protected

  • Nothing leaves your device for us. telly has no server, and no database anywhere except the one in your own browser, so there is no central copy of anyone’s information.
  • Encrypted in transit. The page, and every request telly makes to Google and YouTube, travel over HTTPS, which encrypts them between your browser and the other end. A plain http:// address for this site is redirected to the HTTPS one.
  • Read-only permission. Google limits each pass to the permissions it was issued for, and the only one telly asks for is the one Google describes as “View your YouTube account”.
  • A short-lived pass. Google describes passes of this kind as short-lived, and gives telly nothing longer-lasting to renew one with: a new pass needs you to sign in again. telly sends the pass only to Google: inside each request it makes to YouTube’s data service, and to Google’s sign-in software to hand back when you sign out. telly never puts it in a web address or writes it to a log, and keeps it in storage that belongs to this tab.
  • Kept apart from other sites. What telly saves is held in the storage your browser gives this site. Browsers keep each site’s storage separate, and other websites cannot read it.
  • Kept apart from other accounts. The saved copy is filed under your channel identifier, and signing out removes yours and leaves other accounts’ alone. Someone else who signs in to telly on the same browser has their copy filed under their own identifier.
  • Limits on what the page can load and contact. The page tells your browser to run scripts only from telly’s own address, accounts.google.com and www.youtube.com, and to let those scripts send requests only to telly’s own address and to Google’s addresses for sign-in and for YouTube’s data. The browser refuses anything else these rules cover. They do not cover following a link, or what happens inside YouTube’s player and Google’s sign-in windows, which are YouTube’s and Google’s own pages.
  • Public code. telly’s code is public in the repository linked under “Contact”, together with its threat model, which lists the risks it considers and how each is handled.

What these protections do not cover:

  • telly does not encrypt what it saves on your device. It is protected by your device and your browser: anyone who can use your browser can open this site’s storage and see your channel identifier, the names of the channels you subscribe to, the titles of their uploads, and, while the tab is open, the pass. Someone who copied the pass could use it to view your YouTube account until it runs out or the permission is withdrawn. Your device’s own protections, such as its lock screen and disk encryption, apply to all of it.
  • Google’s sign-in software, and YouTube’s player software once a programme has started, run as part of the page, with the same reach over it as telly’s own code, including the pass and the saved copy. Google and YouTube decide what their software does, and telly does not check it before it runs.

Deleting your information

We hold no copy of your information, so there is nothing for you to ask us to delete. Everything telly keeps is on your own device, and you can remove it yourself:

  • Sign out in the page. This does two things: it hands your permission back to Google, and it removes your saved copy and the pass from your browser. Google notes that a withdrawal can take some time to take full effect.
  • If part of signing out fails, the page tells you which part, with one exception. Each part is carried out regardless of the others, so you are always signed out of the page, and your saved copy is removed even if Google does not take the permission back. If Google does not confirm that it has taken the permission back, you can withdraw it at Google, as described below. The exception: if the request to hand the permission back cannot reach Google, Google’s sign-in software reports it to telly as done, so the page cannot tell you. You can check at Google, as described below. If your browser will not delete the saved copy, clearing this site’s data removes it.
  • After the pass has run out. Once the page has returned to its signed-out state, there is no sign-out control to press, and the pass it had cannot hand the permission back: Google accepts only a pass that is still valid for that. You can check whether telly still has your permission, and withdraw it, at Google, as described below. Signing in and then signing out also hands it back.
  • Clear this site’s data in your browser. Your browser’s settings can delete what this site has stored, which removes what telly has saved, including copies saved for other accounts. It does not hand your permission back to Google.

Apart from handing back the permission, none of this touches anything held by Google or YouTube. Google’s privacy policy, linked below, is where to ask about that.

Taking the permission back at Google

You can withdraw telly’s permission from your Google account at any time, at myaccount.google.com/permissions. telly can then no longer read your YouTube account. The page returns to its signed-out state the next time telly asks YouTube for something and YouTube refuses the pass, and telly asks each time the page is loaded while you are signed in. Doing this does not remove the copy saved on your device. Signing out in the page removes it while the page still shows you as signed in, and clearing this site’s data removes it at any time.

When you are not signed in

Until you sign in, telly shows no programmes, reads nothing from any YouTube account, and asks YouTube for nothing. The page does load Google’s sign-in software when it opens, so Google receives a request from your browser at that point.

Other companies involved

  • Google and YouTube. Signing in, and reading your YouTube account, happen directly between your browser and Google. Google’s privacy policy covers what Google does with those requests.
  • YouTube’s player. Programmes play in YouTube’s own embedded player. When a programme starts, telly loads YouTube’s player software into the page and tells it which video to play. What the player then does, including any advertising it shows, is up to YouTube. telly does not ask for YouTube’s privacy-enhanced mode, which YouTube describes as stopping what you watch in an embedded player from being used to personalise YouTube and its advertising for you. The YouTube Terms of Service apply to that part of using telly.
  • GitHub. telly’s pages are hosted on GitHub Pages. GitHub receives each request for the page, and its documentation states that visitors’ IP addresses are logged and stored for security purposes. What GitHub does with that is covered by the GitHub Privacy Statement. Your YouTube information does not pass through GitHub.

Google API Services User Data Policy

telly’s use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Children

telly is not directed at children, and requires a Google account to sign in to. It asks for no date of birth.
