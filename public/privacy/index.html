Privacy policy

telly · last updated 15 September 2026

telly turns your YouTube subscriptions into television: it builds a day’s schedule out of them and plays them in YouTube’s own embedded player, so you switch it on and see what is on rather than choosing something.

The short version

telly has no server, no database and no backend. It is a single page that runs in your browser. Nothing it reads from your Google account is ever sent to the developer or to anyone else, because there is nowhere for it to be sent.

The one exception is YouTube itself, which plays the programmes. That is covered in its own section below.

What telly asks for

One OAuth scope, and nothing else:

https://www.googleapis.com/auth/youtube.readonly

It allows telly to read the channels you subscribe to and the recent uploads on those channels. It does not allow telly to change anything. telly never posts, never comments, never subscribes or unsubscribes, and never deletes.

What telly does with it

  • Your subscription list becomes the list of channels the schedule can draw on.
  • Each channel’s recent uploads (titles, durations, publication dates, category, view counts, age rating and whether the channel is made for children) decide which of five stations a channel belongs to, and what time of day its programmes go out.
  • Nothing else is requested, and nothing about your viewing, your watch history or your identity is read. telly does not know your name or your email address, because it never asks for them.

Where it is kept

  • The access token lives in memory for about an hour and is then discarded. It is never written to storage, never logged, and never put in a URL. There is no refresh token, because Google issues none to a browser application like this one.
  • The schedule data (the titles, durations and IDs described above) is cached in IndexedDB on your own machine for a day, so that switching the set on again does not spend your API quota. Clearing your site data removes it.
  • Nothing at all is kept anywhere else. There is no account to delete and no copy held by anyone.

How it is protected

telly holds one piece of sensitive data: the OAuth access token that lets it read your subscriptions. These are the measures that protect it, and the schedule data derived from it.

  • In transit. Every request is HTTPS, and the site is served over HTTPS only. The access token travels in an Authorization header, never in a URL, a query string or a referrer, so it cannot be picked up from browser history, a proxy log or a link you share.
  • At rest. The token is never at rest: it is held in memory in the page, discarded when the tab closes or the hour lapses, and written to no storage of any kind. The cached schedule data holds no credentials and is kept by your browser under this site’s own origin, readable only by this site.
  • No server, no second copy. telly has no backend and no database. Nothing read from your Google account is transmitted to the developer or to any third party, because there is nowhere to transmit it to. There is no server to breach, and no operator with access to it.
  • Least privilege. One read-only scope is requested, youtube.readonly, and nothing else. telly cannot write to, post from, subscribe, unsubscribe or delete anything in your account under any circumstances.
  • Containment in the page. The page declares a Content Security Policy naming the only origins allowed to execute script in it (itself, accounts.google.com and www.youtube.com) and forbidding everything else, so injected script has nowhere to load from.
  • Retention and deletion. The token expires within the hour. Cached schedule data expires within a day, and clearing this site’s data in your browser removes it at once. Withdrawing access at Google invalidates the token immediately, and no new one can be issued.
  • How the code is kept honest. telly is open source and every change is public and reviewed. Automated tests assert these properties specifically: that the token reaches no storage, no log, no error object and no URL. A change that breaks one of them fails the build. The source and its dependencies are scanned for known vulnerabilities on every change and weekly.

What telly does not do

  • No analytics, no tracking pixels, no advertising, and no cookies of its own.
  • No selling, sharing or transferring of your data to anybody, for any purpose.
  • No profiling, and no use of your data to train any model.

YouTube’s player

Programmes play in YouTube’s own embedded player, loaded from www.youtube.com. That player is YouTube’s, not telly’s: it may set cookies and collect data about what you watch, exactly as it would on YouTube. telly cannot see or control any of that.

By using telly you are also using YouTube, so the YouTube Terms of Service and the Google Privacy Policy apply to that part of it.

Taking access away

You can revoke telly’s access to your Google account at any time, from Google’s own security settings:

https://security.google.com/settings/security/permissions

Revoking takes effect immediately: telly’s access token stops working and no new one can be issued. To remove the cached schedule data from your machine as well, clear this site’s data in your browser. It expires by itself within a day in any case.

Limited use

telly’s use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Children

telly is not directed at children and requires a Google account to sign in to. It collects nothing from anyone knowingly, children included, because it collects nothing at all.
