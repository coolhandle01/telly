Privacy policy

telly · last updated 20 September 2026

telly builds a day’s television schedule out of the YouTube channels you subscribe to, and plays the programmes in YouTube’s own player. This policy covers the version of telly published at telly.na-n.xyz. It explains what telly reads from your YouTube account, what we do and do not do with it, where it goes, how long it is kept, and how to remove it.

Who is responsible for telly

telly is made and published by one person as a hobby project. There is no company behind it, no telly account to create, and no server of telly’s own. telly runs inside your browser. The person who publishes telly receives none of the information described in this policy.

What we undertake

  • We use what telly reads from your YouTube account for one purpose: building the schedule you watch. We put it to no other use.
  • We do not sell it.
  • We do not share it with, or transfer it to, anyone beyond the companies named in this policy.
  • We ask Google for one permission, the one named below, and for no other.
  • telly loads no analytics software, no tracking software and no advertising.
  • telly sets no cookie of its own.

The permission you give

When you sign in, Google asks you to grant telly one permission:

https://www.googleapis.com/auth/youtube.readonly

It is read-only. It allows telly to read the channels you subscribe to and the videos those channels have uploaded. It does not allow telly to post, comment, subscribe, unsubscribe or delete anything, and it does not give telly your name, your email address or your watch history.

What telly reads from your YouTube account

  • An identifier YouTube uses for your account.
  • The channels you subscribe to, and their names.
  • For each of those channels: the topics YouTube files it under, and its subscriber count where the channel publishes one.
  • For each of those channels, its twenty most recent uploads: the title, how long it runs, when it was published, the category and tags it was uploaded with, its view count, whether YouTube has age-restricted it, whether it is declared as made for children, whether it can be played outside YouTube, and whether it is live.

telly reads nothing else from your account.

What telly does with it

telly sorts the channels you subscribe to between five stations, and gives each station a day of programmes. What it reads decides which station a channel belongs to, and what time of day its uploads go out. The schedule is built in your browser and shown to you, and that is the whole of what the information is used for.

telly keeps no record of what you watch or of what it read, and there is no server of ours to keep one. Google keeps its own record of the requests telly makes with your permission.

Where that information goes

It goes from YouTube to your browser, and stays there. telly has no server to send it to and no database of its own to put it in. No copy of it reaches the person who publishes telly. None of it is sent to any company other than the ones named in this policy.

What is kept on your device, and for how long

  • The schedule. What telly reads from your account is saved in your browser’s storage on this device, so that switching the set on again does not have to fetch it all from YouTube a second time. It is kept for up to 24 hours. After that telly stops using it. The copy stays on your device until telly replaces it, you sign out, or you clear this site’s data.
  • Your sign-in. Google gives telly a pass that lets it read your subscriptions for about an hour. telly keeps that pass in this tab’s own storage, so that reloading the page does not sign you out. It is sent to nobody, and it goes when you close the tab. When the hour is up the pass stops working, telly returns to its signed-out state, and you sign in again.
  • Which account signed in here. telly records in your browser an identifier Google gives it for your account, so that signing in again does not ask you to pick your account out of a list. The identifier is Google’s own, it is different for every app, and it is not your name, your email address or a password. telly reads it from the sign-in itself and never asks YouTube for it. It is removed when you sign out.

telly puts nothing else on your device. Your browser keeps its own copy of the page, as it does for any site.

Who else can read what is on your device

telly does not encrypt any of it, and asks you for nothing before reading it, so anyone who can use your device and open this site can see it. The saved schedule holds the names of the channels you subscribe to and the titles of their recent uploads. It holds no password. While the tab is open, the pass Google gives telly can be read the same way, and of the three things telly keeps it is the only one that grants anything: it reads your subscriptions from your account until its hour is up. The account identifier grants nothing and cannot be used to sign in as you, and it is meaningless to any app but this one, but it does say that this Google account has used telly on this device. Signing out removes all three, and so does clearing this site’s data.

How to remove it

  • Sign out in the page. This does two things: it hands your permission back to Google, and it removes the saved schedule belonging to your account. If someone else has signed in to telly on this device, their saved schedule is left alone. Either half can fail, and the page tells you which one did: a permission that could not be handed back is one you withdraw at Google, and a saved schedule that could not be removed goes with this site’s data.
  • Clear this site’s data in your browser. This removes everything telly has put on your device, including schedules saved by other accounts. It does not hand your permission back to Google.

Neither reaches anything held by Google or YouTube. Google’s privacy policy, linked below, is where to ask about that.

Taking the permission back at Google

You can withdraw telly’s permission from your Google account at any time, at myaccount.google.com/permissions. telly can then no longer read your subscriptions, and the page returns to its signed-out state. Doing this does not remove the schedule already saved on your device. Signing out in the page does that, and so does clearing this site’s data.

When you are not signed in

telly plays a small set of sample programmes built into the page. It reads nothing from any YouTube account, and asks YouTube for none of your data. The page does load Google’s sign-in software when it opens, so Google receives a request from your browser at that point, as it would on any page offering a Google sign-in button.

Other companies involved

  • Google and YouTube. Signing in, and reading your subscriptions, happen directly between your browser and Google. Google’s privacy policy covers what Google does with those requests.
  • YouTube’s player. Programmes play in YouTube’s own embedded player. When a programme starts, telly tells YouTube which video to play. That player belongs to YouTube, and it sets its own cookies and records what you watch, as it does on YouTube itself. telly cannot see or control this. The YouTube Terms of Service apply to that part of using telly.
  • GitHub. telly’s pages are hosted on GitHub Pages. Like any web host, GitHub receives each request for the page, and its documentation states that visitors’ IP addresses are logged and kept for security purposes. What GitHub does with that is covered by the GitHub Privacy Statement.

Your subscriptions do not pass through GitHub. They go between your browser and Google.

Software the page loads from Google and YouTube

Two pieces of software come from outside telly and run as part of the page: Google’s sign-in software, which the page loads when it opens, and YouTube’s player software, which it loads when a programme starts. Both run with the same reach over the page as telly’s own code, which includes the pass Google issues and the schedule saved on your device. Google and YouTube each decide what their software does, and telly cannot check it before it runs. The page loads software from telly’s own address, from Google and from YouTube, and from nowhere else.

The connection

The pages telly serves, and the requests it makes to Google, use HTTPS. A plain http:// address for this site is redirected to the HTTPS one, which means such a request leaves your browser before it is redirected.

Google API Services User Data Policy

telly’s use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Children

telly is not directed at children, and requires a Google account to sign in to. telly asks for no profile information, and reads no name, email address or date of birth.

Changes to this policy

When this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it. Every previous version of it stays public in the repository linked below, so you can see what changed and when.
